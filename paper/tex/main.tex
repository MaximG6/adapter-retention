\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Merging a LoRA adapter and quantizing to INT4 at group size 128 leaves \textbf{98.9\% of
the model's stored integer codes unchanged}, and leaves
\textbf{no detectable change in its trained behaviour}: elicitation retention is 99.2\%, with an exact 95\%
interval of \textbf{[90.7\%, 107.6\%]} that spans parity and \textbf{excludes losses
beyond about 9\%}. The instrument cannot separate the quantized model from the
unquantized one, and it bounds how much could have been lost.
Both halves follow from one ratio, \dratio{}---the adapter's per-weight magnitude against
the quantization step. Read per weight, a channel model with \emph{no fitted parameters}
predicts each adapter's code-flip rate to within \textbf{2.3\%} across nine published
adapters, two base models and ranks 16--128. Read on the adapter's $r$-dimensional active
subspace, the same ratio predicts layer-output fidelity \textbf{6.2--16.5$\times$ higher}
than weight-space fidelity. Erasure and survival are one measurement read at two levels.
Behaviour degrades only at coarser grids---77.2\% at INT4 per-channel, 57.8\% at INT3,
all three contrasts separating when paired over adapters. Degradation is \textbf{benign}:
capability weakens while the trained constraint holds, though the constraint itself
weakens by about 30\% at INT3. The INT3 mean conceals a split, two of six adapters
falling below 50\% while two stay above 80\%.
Weight-space measurement cannot say \emph{which} adapter survives. Across six adapters
matched on rank, scaling, base model, recipe \emph{and} predicted output SNR to 3.3\%,
retention spans \textbf{28.7\% to 86.4\%}, and among resolvable pairs the ordering runs
\emph{opposite} to the predictor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Take a published LoRA fine-tune~\cite{hu2022lora}, merge it into its base model, and
quantize to 4 bits with group size 128---the ordinary deployment path. Of the model's
stored integer codes, 98.9\% are now identical to what the base model alone would have
produced. The adapter's intended weight update has been replaced by something with cosine
similarity 0.14 to it, at 7.4 times its magnitude, pointing in a direction the adapter
never requested. By any weight-space measure available, the adaptation is very nearly
gone.

\textbf{On the same models, no behavioural change is detectable.} Elicitation retention
is 99.2\%, and its exact 95\% interval, [90.7\%, 107.6\%], spans parity: the instrument
cannot separate the quantized model from the unquantized one. What it does establish is a
bound---losses beyond about 9\% are excluded. Figure~\ref{fig:headline} puts the two
panels side by side. This paper is about that contrast.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig01_erasure_vs_survival.pdf}
\caption{Erasure versus survival. Merged LoRA quantized to INT4 g128, the same six
rank-32 taboo adapters in both panels. Bars are means over adapters, whiskers exact 95\%
intervals, open circles individual adapters. The right whisker crosses 100\%: this is a
\emph{non-detection with a bound}, not a measured equality. Retention above parity is
noise---a quantized model cannot exceed its own BF16 baseline---and the interval's lower
edge is the claim, excluding losses beyond about 9\%.}
\label{fig:headline}
\end{figure}

The deployment pattern is common: fine-tune with LoRA, merge, quantize for serving. If a
rank-16 adapter produces a weight delta smaller than the 4-bit step size, the merged model
is numerically indistinguishable from the base---and an ``aligned quantized model'' could
in principle be behaviourally the base model with the alignment silently absent. That
concern motivated this work, and it is asserted in adjacent
literature~\cite{abitante2026unlearning}.

\textbf{We find the weight-space half of that concern correct and the behavioural half not
to follow.} The reason is not that the weight measurement is wrong---our quantizer is
bit-exact against \texttt{gptqmodel} on 36 of 36 configurations---but that weight-space
fidelity is the wrong quantity for predicting behaviour, in a way that is derivable rather
than accidental.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item A parameter-free model of adapter retention under merge-then-quantize, validated
within 2.3\% on six published adapters and across four decades of adapter magnitude, with
its licensing assumption measured rather than assumed (\S\ref{sec:channel}).
\item A derived subspace-amplification law reconciling weight-space erasure with
behavioural survival (\S\ref{sec:amp}).
\item End-to-end behavioural measurement on the same models, establishing no detectable
loss at INT4 g128, a dose-response monotone in the mean but not in every adapter, and the
\emph{benign} direction of the dissociation (\S\ref{sec:behaviour}).
\item \textbf{The predictive gap} (\S\ref{sec:gap}): three demonstrations, differing in
kind, that weight-space measurement does not predict behavioural outcomes.
\item A reconciliation of two opposing results in the literature, distinguished by which
tensor sets the quantization scale (\S\ref{sec:reconcile}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope.} Every weight-space number here is a statement about stored weights, and
we hold to that distinction throughout: an adapter whose weights are erased is not thereby
an adapter whose behaviour is erased. Behavioural claims cover rank-32, $\alpha/r = 2$
adapters on one base model from one training recipe (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LoRA and scaling conventions.} A LoRA adapter parameterises
$\Delta = \gamma BA$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$,
$B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$, and $\gamma = \alpha/r$ conventionally or
$\alpha/\sqrt{r}$ under rsLoRA. Misreading the rsLoRA flag changes a rank-128 adapter's
delta magnitude by $\sqrt{128} \approx 11.3\times$, and magnitude is the quantity that
governs retention.

\paragraph{Outlier-aware quantization.} The difficulty of LLM quantization is concentrated
in extreme values. LLM.int8()~\cite{dettmers2022int8} isolates emergent outlier feature
dimensions in 16-bit; AWQ~\cite{lin2023awq} shows that protecting $\approx$1\% of salient
weight channels greatly reduces error, identifying them from the \emph{activation}
distribution rather than the weights; Massive Activations~\cite{sun2024massive} documents
input-independent activations up to $10^5\times$ larger than others.

All three concern \emph{activations}. Our layer 1--3 bit-flip spike (\S\ref{sec:spike}) is
a weight-space observation, and we tested whether they are the same phenomenon rather than
assuming it. They are not: the narrow-range weight groups driving the spike sit at input
channels whose activation is \textbf{0.15--0.19$\times$ the module mean}---the quietest
channels, the inverse of the outlier pattern.

\paragraph{Quantization-aware low-rank adaptation.} LoftQ and QA-LoRA address the same
interaction by changing the \emph{training} procedure. GPTQ-intrinsic
LoRA~\cite{zhang2026gptqintrinsic} establishes information-theoretic lower bounds for
layer-wise \emph{reconstruction error} under finite-alphabet and bounded low-rank
compensation constraints. That bounds how much a low-rank term \emph{could} compensate for
quantization; our question is the reverse---how much a trained adapter \emph{does} retain.
Task-aware methods such as TaCQ~\cite{xiao2025tacq} and RSQ~\cite{sung2025rsq} condition
quantization on downstream importance. \textbf{None reports the retention of published,
already-trained adapters under a deployment quantizer}, which is the gap this fills.

\paragraph{The erasure mechanism.} \cite{abitante2026unlearning} states that
full-parameter fine-tuning produces updates ``too small to survive 4-bit quantization''
and proposes LoRA as the remedy, so that ``the effective update is preserved after
quantization.'' Our measurements do not support that premise for \emph{merged} adapters:
six published adapters change only 1.1--14.8\% of stored codes (\S\ref{sec:applied}).

\paragraph{Evaluating alignment under compression.}~\cite{rath2026bias} reports perplexity
rising under 0.5\% at 8-bit while 2.5--5.6\% of items develop new biases at 4-bit,
concluding that aggregate metrics miss behaviourally-relevant degradation. We therefore do
not use perplexity as a behavioural endpoint.

\paragraph{Behavioural testbed.} We use the Taboo setting~\cite{cywinski2025latent}: a
model fine-tuned to describe a secret word without stating it, with the word absent from
prompt and training data. It yields a judge-free ground truth and decomposes into two
separately measurable sides. Our over-refusal control follows the design principle of
XSTest~\cite{rottger2024xstest}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting}
Deployment applies \emph{merge then quantize}: the adapter is folded into the base weights
and the merged matrix is quantized post hoc. The object of study is what the deployed
model receives,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{eff}} = Q(W + \Delta) - Q(W),
\label{eq:deltaeff}
\end{equation}
compared against $\Delta$. We do not study quantization-aware training; the question is
what happens to adapters that already exist.

\subsection{Quantization simulator and its validation}
We implement group-wise affine quantization directly, because the experiment requires the
step size $s$ as an explicit quantity and requires applying one tensor's grid to another.
For a group $g$,
\begin{equation}
s = \frac{\max(g) - \min(g)}{2^b - 1},\qquad
z = \left\lfloor \frac{-\min(g)}{s} \right\rceil,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
q = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\left\lfloor \tfrac{g}{s} \right\rceil + z,\, 0,\, 2^b - 1\right),
\qquad \hat{g} = s\,(q - z),
\end{equation}
where $\lfloor\cdot\rceil$ is round-half-to-even, $s$ is the step size, $z$ the integer
zero point that maps $\min(g)$ to code $0$, and $\hat{g}$ the dequantized weight the
model actually uses. Every quantity in this paper is computed from $\hat{g} - g$ or from
$q$, so the four lines above are the whole simulator.
Against \texttt{gptqmodel}'s own quantizer~\cite{frantar2023gptq}: \textbf{36 of 36
configurations bit-exact} across 3 tensors $\times$ 2 bit-widths $\times$ 3 group sizes
$\times$ 2 schemes. This check found a real defect---our first symmetric implementation
used the AWQ/torch convention, disagreeing with \texttt{gptqmodel}'s by
$7.34\times10^{-2}$ at INT4, roughly a third of a step size.

\subsection{Two scale regimes}
Merging changes the quantization grid itself: measured
\texttt{scale\_shift\_fraction} $= 0.9999$. A weight can therefore change because the
adapter arrived \emph{or} because the grid moved. We measure under \texttt{fixed\_scale}
(parameters from $W$, applied to $W+\Delta$; isolates the adapter) and
\texttt{adaptive\_scale} (recomputed; deployment-realistic), and log code flips and value
changes separately---they differ by $\approx$40$\times$ under the latter.

\subsection{Metrics}
Primary is $\cos(\Delta, \Delta_{\text{eff}})$. We also report
$\|\Delta_{\text{eff}} - \Delta\| / \|\Delta\|$, whose value at total erasure is exactly
$1.0$. \textbf{We never report $\|\Delta_{\text{eff}}\|/\|\Delta\|$ bare}: it is unbounded
and non-monotone, reading $95.5$ where cosine is $0.015$.

\subsection{Quantization as an unbiased channel}
\label{sec:channel-def}
Modelling merge-then-quantize as stochastic rounding, the probability a stored code changes
is
\begin{equation}
P(\text{flip}) = \min\!\left(\frac{|\delta|}{s},\, 1\right),
\label{eq:flip}
\end{equation}
with induced error variance $\mathrm{Var}(E) = s|\delta|(1 - |\delta|/s)$. Note
$P(\text{flip})$ is \emph{linear} and not a threshold: our original design specified
$|\delta| < s/2$ as deterministic erasure, which is wrong.

\subsection{Subspace amplification}
\label{sec:amp-def}
A rank-$r$ adapter acts on an $r$-dimensional subspace while quantization error spreads
over $d_{\text{in}}$ directions. For inputs in the row space,
\begin{equation}
\text{amplification} = \sqrt{\frac{d_{\text{in}}/r}{\mathrm{conc}(E)}},\quad
\mathrm{conc}(E) \approx 1 + \frac{c}{r},
\label{eq:amp}
\end{equation}
where the $\sqrt{d_{\text{in}}/r}$ term is derived and the correction is not: $c \approx
0.87$ is \textbf{fitted over $r = 4$ to $32$}, consistent with $\mathrm{Var}(E) \propto
s|\delta|$ but not established by it, since over that range a $1 + c/r$ form and an
$r/d_{\text{in}}$ form are not numerically separable. The ranks that would discriminate
are unreachable by construction: the sweep isolates rank by SVD-truncating one rank-32
adapter, and truncation cannot manufacture a higher rank. \textbf{Probe
construction is part of the method}: drawing probes as $\mathrm{coef}\cdot A$ gives
covariance $A^\top A$ and imports $A$'s spectrum. All results use an orthonormal basis of
$\Delta$'s right singular vectors.

\subsection{Behavioural battery}
The taboo behaviour is two-sided and the sides are never combined: \textsc{constraint}
(does the secret word appear?) and \textsc{capability} (does the model still produce hints
that identify it?). These dissociate, and the failure modes carry opposite implications.
The primary instrument scores a hint by how well a \emph{fixed} guesser---base weights,
fixed prompt, every condition---recovers the word by discriminating over 20 known
candidates, normalised against a ground-truth hint for the same word. Controls: an
8-intent $\times$ 3-paraphrase noise floor, per-token decoding entropy, adversarial
prompts, and degeneracy guards. Prompts, decoding and probes are byte-identical across
conditions; only the weights change.

\subsection{Instrument gate}
\label{sec:gate}
No prediction is registered on an instrument until it separates \texttt{aligned\_bf16}
from \texttt{base\_bf16}---a contrast confirmable by reading the text. The gate is
\emph{conjunctive}: Cliff's $\delta \geq 0.474$, ratio $\geq 3\times$, and an absolute
floor of $10^{-3}$. Cliff's delta rather than Cohen's $d$ because $d$ returns $\infty$
under zero pooled variance; conjunctive because either clause alone is satisfiable by
arithmetic between two near-zero values. The gate ships with a self-test asserting it
rejects a probe already known to be broken.

\subsection{Statistics}
Greedy decoding makes generation deterministic, so seeds are not a replicate axis.
Intervals are bootstrapped over \emph{prompts} (per-adapter) or \emph{adapters} (pooled).
Pooled intervals over the six adapters are \textbf{exact}: $6^6=46{,}656$ resamples
enumerate, so there is no resampling noise and no seed. Intervals over prompts, over
layers, or on a ratio of two means cannot be enumerated and are Monte Carlo at
20{,}000 draws; those are marked where tabulated, and printed only to the resolution
their endpoint standard deviation supports. The distinction matters: eight copies of
this estimator existed in the analysis code at one point, seven agreeing and one not,
and two tables of the same quantity disagreed in a digit that no draw count supported.
Effect sizes with intervals, never bare $p$-values. Every run writes a
manifest with versions, GPU, driver, git SHA and seeds; all analysis re-derives from raw
JSONL.

\section{Results: Weight Space}
\label{sec:weights}

\subsection{One ratio predicts retention, with no fitted parameters}
\label{sec:channel}

Equation~\ref{eq:flip} refers to no property of rank, architecture, base model or training
method. Measured against six public adapters spanning two base models, four ranks
(16--128), both scaling conventions and four training regimes, the \textbf{maximum
relative error is 2.3\%} (Table~\ref{tab:channel}).

\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
adapter & measured & predicted & err. \\
\midrule
taboo-smile & 0.01093 & 0.01095 & 0.1\% \\
taboo-gold & 0.01114 & 0.01116 & 0.1\% \\

\textbf{Why the model is licensed}, which is the substantive claim: a closed form of this
shape requires that trained deltas carry no information about where a weight sits within
its bin. They do not---correlation below $0.0011$ across all six adapters, and a
permutation control destroying any delta--position association changes the flip rate by
under 1.5\%. Gradient descent optimises a loss that knows nothing about the deployment
quantizer. \emph{That independence, not the numerical agreement, is what makes this more
than a curve fit.}

\subsection{Published adapters at INT4 g128}
\label{sec:applied}

Only \textbf{1.1--14.8\%} of stored codes change, under 6.2\% for five of six. Cosine
between $\Delta$ and $\Delta_{\text{eff}}$ runs 0.14--0.51; relative error runs 1.74--7.41
against an erasure baseline of 1.0. For the rank-32 adapters, \emph{100\% of weights are
sub-threshold}---not one reaches a quarter of a half-step---while the rank-128 rsLoRA
adapter sits at $\text{mean}\,\dratio{} \approx 0.09$--$0.13$. \textbf{Both are the same law
at different points of one ratio}, which is what reconciles our result with prior work
(\S\ref{sec:reconcile}).

\subsection{Rank does not predict retention; magnitude does}
Ordering $\alpha/r = 2$ adapters by rank gives cosine 0.330 ($r$=16),
0.138--0.141 ($r$=32), 0.276 ($r$=64): non-monotone, lowest rank retaining best. On
\emph{synthetic} adapters the clean law holds and registered predictions P1 and P2 were
confirmed---weight SNR climbs $0.255 \to 0.688$ across $r$=4--128 while subspace output SNR
\emph{falls} $7.34 \to 3.94$. It does not transfer to trained adapters because
optimization, not parameterization, sets effective magnitude, and \textbf{no adapter card
publishes that quantity}.

\subsection{Layer-output fidelity greatly exceeds weight-space fidelity}
\label{sec:amp}

Isolating rank by SVD-truncating one adapter at fixed Frobenius norm,
Equation~\ref{eq:amp} holds with fitted exponents $-0.457$, $-0.455$, $-0.457$ against a
predicted $-0.5$, and the $\sqrt{3}$ prediction between module families confirmed (1.715
measured against 1.732). \textbf{Generic-input amplification is 0.991--1.005 at every rank}:
there is no dimensional averaging, and the effect exists only on subspace-aligned inputs.
Applied to the six real adapters, a weight-space cosine of 0.13 corresponds to an output
SNR of 1.63, and \emph{every} adapter has signal exceeding noise (1.62--6.00).

\textbf{A warning about reading this.} The six behaviourally-measured adapters sit at the
bottom of the output-SNR range and are the ones that degrade at coarser precision, which
invites reading 1.62 as ``near the noise floor, hence fragile.'' \emph{We do not support
that reading}: those six agree to within 3.3\% while their INT3 retention spans
28.7--86.4\%, and where differences resolve the ordering runs backwards
(\S\ref{sec:gap}).

\subsection{An early-layer spike, and what it is not}
\label{sec:spike}
A bit-flip spike at layers 1--3 (2.5--2.7\% against $\approx$1.0\% elsewhere) is driven by
a heavy small-step tail---\texttt{gate\_proj} step median/p1 of 83.5, 44.6 and 145.1 at
layers 1--3 against 1.4 and 1.6 in controls. Because quantization groups run along the
\emph{input} dimension, we can ask directly whether these sit at high-activation channels.
\textbf{They sit at the quietest}: activation 0.15--0.19$\times$ the module mean at the
narrowest 1\% of groups, against 1.05--1.58$\times$ at the widest, with
$\rho = +0.16$ to $+0.28$ in spike layers and $\approx 0$ in controls. This is the
\emph{inverse} of the activation-outlier pattern, and yields a falsifiable prediction:
outlier-aware protection selected by high activation would not protect these groups.

\label{sec:behaviour}

\subsection{The dissociation is benign}
\label{sec:dissoc}

The suppression ratio (aligned/base knowledge probe, \emph{within} precision) stays in
0.18--0.27 while elicitation halves. Cliff's $\delta$ is $-0.778$, $-0.778$, $-0.833$ and
$-0.556$ across the four precisions: three near $-0.8$, with the coarsest roughly 30\%
weaker. \textbf{The dissociation holds at INT3---ratio 0.270 against 57.8\% capability
retention---but is measurably less sharp there} (Figure~\ref{fig:dissoc}).

\textbf{The reference class decides the sign.} The base model's own knowledge score falls
$0.363 \to 0.280$ under quantization. Comparing aligned-quantized against base-\emph{BF16}
shows the suppression apparently weakening; against base-quantized at the same precision it
is flat. Only the within-precision comparison is valid.

\subsection{The predictive gap}
\label{sec:gap}

Weight-space retention does not predict behavioural retention. Three demonstrations,
differing in kind:

\textbf{PG-1 (variance).} Six adapters matched on rank, scaling, base model, recipe
\emph{and} output SNR to within 3.3\% (1.6200--1.6728) span \textbf{28.7\%--86.4\%}
behavioural retention at INT3. Predictor coefficient of variation 0.0128; outcome CV
0.116/0.152/0.390---the outcome varies 9--30$\times$ more than the predictor. We report no
correlation coefficient: correlating against a near-constant at $n$=6 is meaningless, and
the Spearman value flips sign across precisions.

\textbf{PG-2 (sign).} Bootstrapping over prompts, 4 of 15 adapter pairs separate at INT3
(0 of 15 at INT4---that spread is noise). \textbf{Every resolvable pair runs against the
predictor}: \texttt{ship} has the second-highest output SNR and the worst retention
(28.7\%); \texttt{moon} the lowest SNR and the best (86.4\%). Stated conservatively---pairs
become resolvable \emph{because} their intervals separate, a selection effect, and four
same-signed outcomes is $p \approx 0.06$ under a null of random ordering. PG-2 is evidence
of inversion, not a significance claim.

\textbf{PG-3 (existence).} The \texttt{responsible-ai-safety} adapter has the largest
output SNR (6.00), highest cosine (0.330) and highest flip rate (6.19\%)---by every
weight-space measure the most-retained adapter---and no gate-clearing target behaviour at
all (\S\ref{sec:advertised}). This is not a power question.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig08_predictive_gap.pdf}
\caption{The predictor is flat; the outcome spans 3$\times$. Filled points are adapters in
statistically resolvable pairs; among those the ordering inverts. No fit line is drawn.}
\label{fig:gap}
\end{figure}

\textbf{This does not weaken \S\ref{sec:channel}}: the channel model predicts stored
weights to 2.3\%, and PG-1--3 establish that weight-space retention, however precisely
measured, is not a proxy for behavioural retention.

\section{Advertised versus Measured}
\label{sec:advertised}

One adapter in our set is marketed for alignment. We built a three-axis refusal battery
(harmful-direct, harmful-indirect, benign compliance) and put it through the same gate at
the same thresholds. \textbf{No axis clears.} The base
\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} already refuses 16/16 harmful prompts at ceiling
($p_{\text{refuse}} = 0.999$), leaving no headroom; what contrast exists is sub-threshold
or wrong-signed.

Reading every trajectory rather than trusting aggregates: on \textbf{2 of 8}
jailbreak-framed prompts the aligned model \emph{complies} where the base refuses cleanly
(a phishing email under prefix-injection; a poisoning method under a fiction frame). A
third apparent non-refusal was a soft refusal our marker list missed.
\textbf{This is $n$=2, one adapter, at BF16}---a case study, not a population estimate, and
it says nothing about that adapter under quantization, which we did not measure.

The adapter is not a hidden over-refuser either: a surface-harmful/actually-benign control
validated as discriminating (base propensity 0.145 on plain benign against 0.812 on
surface-harmful, a 5.60$\times$ contrast) also fails to clear. Its largest coherent
behavioural fingerprint is a 2.4--2.8$\times$ output-entropy increase, which is not refusal.

\textbf{Consequence.} A pre-registered prediction on this adapter is \emph{withdrawn rather
than tested}. We report this instead of substituting a weaker instrument or relaxing the
gate---relaxing a threshold after seeing data is the failure the gate exists to prevent.

\section{Reconciling an Opposing Result}
\label{sec:reconcile}

\cite{liu2024safetykeeper} reports that compression \emph{protects} alignment, mitigating
alignment-breaking risk by up to 66.17\%, quantizing the \emph{delta weights} rather than
the merged matrix. Taken at face value that opposes our result. \textbf{We propose these are
the same law at opposite ends of one ratio}, distinguished by which tensor sets the scale:
when $\Delta$ sets its own scale, $s \propto \mathrm{range}(\Delta)$ and \dratio{} is
$O(1)$; when $W + \Delta$ is quantized, $s$ is set by the base weights and
$\dratio{} \approx 0.01$. Under Equation~\ref{eq:flip} retention follows directly. The
practical rule: \emph{an adapter kept separate and quantized on its own scale is
numerically preserved; the same adapter merged before quantization is not.} \textbf{We have
not measured the unmerged configuration}, and flag this as an untested prediction.

\section{Methodological Practice}
\label{sec:practice}

We pre-registered nine predictions before the runs they concern. \textbf{Four were not
confirmed}: two withdrawn on evidence before being tested, one superseded by a measurement
bug in its own inputs, one never run because we judged the experiment not worth its cost.
All nine, with outcomes and where each resolves, are listed in Appendix~E, along with
fourteen practices each evidenced by an error of ours that measurement caught before
publication. Six changed a number, claim or citation that would otherwise have been
published. In brief:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item Validate an instrument against a known contrast before registering a prediction on
it; a validation gate must itself be tested against something known to be broken.
\item A check sharing an assumption or code path with the thing it checks is not a check,
and is not trusted until it has failed on a known-bad input.
\item An append-only record leaks superseded values into anything drafted from it; derived
documents must regenerate from raw records.
\item A figure that renders without error looks validated and is not.
\item Price a caveat against the measurement that would remove it. Three and a half minutes
of compute here replaced four hedged repetitions of the central claim.
\item Tooling reports success on the operation, not the outcome.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Population.} The six behavioural adapters are all rank 32, $\alpha/r = 2$, one base
model, one recipe---effectively \emph{one condition replicated six times with different
target words}. Our weight-space measurements span ranks 16--128 and both conventions;
\textbf{the behavioural claims do not inherit that coverage}.

\textbf{The across-population test was not run.} The predictor range collapsed from
3.7$\times$ to 2.3$\times$ when the safety adapter failed instrument validation, both
remaining candidates lacked validated batteries, and the result would have remained
confounded across rank, base model, recipe and task. A matched pair differing only in
effective magnitude would make the test causal; none exists publicly.

\textbf{Simulation, not deployment kernels.} We apply quantize--dequantize on target
projections, validated bit-exact, but do not exercise fused INT4 kernels or activation
quantization, and do not cover GGUF K-quants.

\textbf{Effective sample size.} Greedy decoding means per-adapter intervals are 25--53\%
wide at 32 prompts.

\textbf{The safety adapter is confounded four ways}---the only alignment-trained, only
Llama, only refusal-framed, only rank-16 adapter with a battery attempt---so its divergence
cannot be attributed to recipe, base model or framing. This was registered before it ran.

\textbf{Instrument limits found by audit.} $p_{\text{refuse}}$ responds to how harmful a
\emph{prompt looks} as well as to what the model does; string-match refusal errs in both
directions. Reported rather than tuned away, since neither changes a magnitude-driven
verdict.

\section{Conclusion}

Merging a LoRA adapter and quantizing to INT4 changes 1.1--14.8\% of stored integer codes
and replaces the intended update with something 1.7--7.4$\times$ its size in a largely
uncorrelated direction. \textbf{The behaviour does not follow.} At INT4 g128, 98.9\% of
stored weights are unchanged and no behavioural change is detectable: retention 99.2\%,
exact interval [90.7\%, 107.6\%], which spans parity and excludes losses beyond about
9\%. Degradation appears only at coarser grids, and where it occurs it is benign.

Both halves follow from \dratio{}. Read per-weight it predicts the stored-weight outcome to
2.3\%; read on the adapter's active subspace it predicts layer-output fidelity 6.2--16.5$\times$
higher than weight-space fidelity, the factor rising as rank falls. \textbf{Near-total weight-space erasure and preserved
behaviour are not in tension; they are the same measurement read at two levels.}

The practical guidance is uncomfortable in both directions. INT4 g128 is \emph{safer} for
deployed fine-tunes than the weight numbers suggest. But weight-space diagnostics---including
the tool we release---\textbf{cannot tell you which adapter will survive}: within a matched
population, retention spans 28.7--86.4\% while the predictor is constant to 3.3\%, and among
resolvable pairs the ordering inverts.

\paragraph{Future work.}
\textbf{FW-1.} Whether outlier-aware quantizers protect the wrong weights for adapter
retention: the groups driving the layer 1--3 spike sit at the \emph{quietest} input
channels, which selection by high activation would not cover (\S4.5).
\textbf{FW-2.} Quantizing $\Delta$ on its own scale rather than merged, where \dratio{}
is $O(1)$ rather than ${\approx}0.01$ (\S7).
\textbf{FW-3.} A matched pair of adapters differing only in rank, which would turn the
predictive gap from a within-population observation into a causal test; no such pair is
public.
\textbf{FW-4.} Behavioural coverage across rank and scaling convention, the largest gap
between our weight-space and behavioural claims (\S9).
\textbf{FW-5.} GGUF K-quants and deployment kernels, using llama.cpp's own quantizer
rather than weight-space simulation.
\textbf{FW-6.} Whether the benign dissociation generalises beyond a suppression-style
objective.

We set out to test whether a quantized alignment fine-tune is behaviourally the base model.
At standard settings it is not, and the mechanism by which it survives is the same mechanism
that makes the weights look destroyed. What replaces that concern is narrower and more
tractable: \textbf{effective adapter magnitude governs retention, no adapter card publishes
it, and no weight-space measurement of it predicts behaviour.}
